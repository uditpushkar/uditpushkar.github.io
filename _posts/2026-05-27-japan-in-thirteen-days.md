## The Plan

Japan is not a trip. Japan is an assault. A very polite, very punctual, extremely well-packaged assault on the senses. One moment you are standing under a thousand torii gates contemplating the meaning of civilization, and the next moment you are inside a gaming tower in Tokyo pretending to be Lewis Hamilton in a simulator. Balance, as they say, is important. <!--more-->

This is a thirteen-day Japan itinerary covering Tokyo, Kyoto, Kanazawa, Osaka, Nara and Koyasan. It is not a slow trip. It is not a spiritual retreat. It is a carefully packed suitcase of temples, sushi, ramen, neon, coffee, trains, shrines, markets, viewpoints and the occasional planned collapse.

The broad idea is simple: start with Tokyo madness, move to Kyoto grace, add a Kanazawa detour, and end with Osaka food and day trips. If one survives with knees intact and Google Maps still cooperating, it should be a very good trip.

## Day 1 - 28 April: Tokyo Begins with Shibuya

The first day begins with checking into the hotel in Tokyo and then surrendering to Shibuya. This is a sensible place to start because Shibuya is Tokyo announcing itself loudly. The crossing is obviously mandatory. One does not go to Tokyo and avoid Shibuya Crossing. That would be like going to Agra and saying, "No thanks, I will skip the Taj."

The plan is to move from the crossing to **Shibuya Sky** around sunset. This is the kind of touristy thing that remains touristy because it works. Tokyo from above, light fading, buildings starting to glow, everyone silently agreeing that urban civilization is both beautiful and mildly insane.

After that comes **Miyashita Park**, followed by **Yoyogi Park**, **Meiji Shrine**, **Harajuku**, **Takeshita Street**, and **Omotesando**. This is a day of contrasts: shrine silence, youth fashion, luxury streets, doughnuts, coffee, and human traffic moving like an organized river.

Food note: **Higuma Doughnuts + specialty coffee** in Omotesando, and **Kaiten Sushi Ginza Onodera** for dinner. Start strong. Jet lag can complain later.

## Day 2 - 29 April: Asakusa by Day, Shinjuku by Night

Day 2 is split neatly into culture and chaos.

Morning belongs to **Asakusa**. The main stop is **Senso-ji Temple**, one of those places where Tokyo suddenly remembers that it was old long before it became futuristic. **Nakamise Street** follows, with the usual temptation of snacks, souvenirs, and small purchases that appear unnecessary until one is already billing them.

Then comes **Kappabashi**, the kitchen and cutlery district. Knife town, basically. Very dangerous for people who believe they are one Japanese knife away from becoming better cooks.

Night belongs to **Shinjuku**. **Kabukicho**, the famous entertainment district, is best seen after dark. The 3D cat is on the list because apparently even digital cats in Japan have better public presence than most elected representatives. **Omoide Yokocho** follows, with its busy noodle joints and narrow lanes. **Hanazono Shrine** adds a quieter note before dinner at **Fuunji**, the noodle place that must be respected.

## Day 3 - 30 April: Akihabara, Ginza and Tokyo Tower

Day 3 begins with **Akihabara**, the holy land of anime, gaming, collectibles and sensory overload. **Kotobukiya** is marked as a must. This is not shopping. This is anthropology with a wallet.

The day then shifts to Ginza and central Tokyo. The list includes **Hie Shrine**, **Tsukiji Outer Market** for tamagoyaki omelette, **Azabudai Hills Sky Lobby**, **Tokyo Tower**, and then **Red Tokyo Tower** for VR, simulation racing and adult gaming. This is perhaps the most Tokyo sentence ever written: shrine, omelette, skyline, tower, VR racing.

Dinner is at **Yoroniku Tokyo**. A serious meal at the end of a deeply unserious amount of walking.

## Day 4 - 1 May: TeamLab, Imperial Palace and Recovery

Day 4 is wisely designed as a lighter day. **TeamLab** and the **Imperial Palace** are the main items, but the important phrase is "rest day".

Every itinerary needs this. The amateur mistake is assuming enthusiasm is a substitute for cartilage. It is not. By Day 4, the feet will begin filing formal complaints. This is the day to move slower, eat properly, sit somewhere without guilt, and let Tokyo pass by for a while.

## Day 5 - 2 May: Mount Fuji Day Trip

Day 5 is the Mount Fuji day trip. This is the postcard day. The mountain may show itself. The mountain may decide it is above human expectation and hide behind clouds. Either way, one goes.

The plan is simple: leave Tokyo, do the Fuji circuit, return to Tokyo. No heroics. No attempt to squeeze three more neighbourhoods after returning. Fuji gets the day.

## Day 6 - 3 May: Shinkansen to Kyoto

Day 6 is transition day. Travel to Kyoto by **Shinkansen**, check into the hotel, and then step into a different Japan.

First stop: **Nishiki Market**. This is dangerous territory for people who think they are "just browsing". Food markets are never browsing. They are slow-motion surrender.

Evening goes to **Gion**, the traditional entertainment district. **Hanami-koji Street** and tea houses are on the route, with the possibility of spotting a geisha. The correct approach here is restraint. Observe, do not behave like a paparazzi uncle with a power bank.

## Day 7 - 4 May: Arashiyama and the Golden Temple

Day 7 begins early with a train from Kyoto to **Arashiyama**. Early is important because bamboo groves and crowds do not mix well. The **Bamboo Grove** is first, followed by **Tenryu-ji**, a Zen Buddhist temple, and **Senko-ji**, a quieter forested temple.

Later comes **Kinkaku-ji**, the golden pavilion and former shogun retirement villa. One must respect anyone whose retirement plan involved a gold-covered structure. Modest living, clearly.

Dinner is at **Ramen Sen No Kaze**. After temples and walking, ramen is not food. It is infrastructure.

## Day 8 - 5 May: Fushimi Inari, Temples and Pontocho

Day 8 is shrine and temple heavy. **Fushimi Inari Shrine** comes first, with the famous thousand torii gates. This is one of those places where even people who claim not to take photos will suddenly become documentary photographers.

Then come **Nanzen-ji** and **Eikan-do**, both excellent for a slower Kyoto day. **Pontocho** is planned for the evening, with Kobe beef as the reward. **Nijo Castle** is optional, and so is a day trip to **Kibune and Kurama** depending on energy levels.

This is a sensible day to keep flexible. Kyoto rewards wandering. It also punishes overconfidence.

## Day 9 - 6 May: Kanazawa Day Trip

Day 9 is a Kanazawa day trip from Kyoto. The plan includes the **D. T. Suzuki Museum**, **Kanazawa Castle**, and then dinner decisions between **Sushi Ippei** and **Menya Taiga** for miso ramen.

Kanazawa is the elegant detour. Less noisy than Tokyo, less obvious than Kyoto, and refined in that understated Japanese way where everything looks simple until one realizes it is probably the result of 400 years of discipline.

## Day 10 - 7 May: Osaka Arrival

Day 10 begins with travel to Osaka and hotel check-in. Osaka should not be treated like Tokyo's younger cousin. It has its own personality: louder, warmer, hungrier.

The list starts with **Namba Yasaka Shrine**, then **Osaka Castle**, **Amerikamura**, and **Umeda Sky Building** for the night view. Dinner is **okonomiyaki at Okaru**, which is exactly the kind of dinner Osaka deserves.

This is the city where dieting should be legally suspended.

## Day 11 - 8 May: Nara Day Trip

Day 11 is Nara. The headline stop is **Todai-ji Temple**, followed by a climb up **Mount Wakakusa**. There are also mochi shops, because civilization must continue.

Optional stops include **Isuien Garden**, **Nara National Museum**, and **Naramachi**. The plan then returns to the Osaka hotel.

Nara is a good day trip because it gives scale. Temples, hills, gardens and old streets make one realize that Japan is not only efficient trains and neon signs. It is also wood, stone, silence and time.

## Day 12 - 9 May: Koyasan Day Trip

Day 12 is **Koyasan**, and this is likely to be one of the most atmospheric days of the trip. The plan includes **Daimon Gate**, **Kongobu-ji**, **Banryutei Rock Garden**, **Okunoin Cemetery**, and the **Kobo Daishi Museum**.

Koyasan should be done slowly. This is not a checklist place. It is a place where the air itself seems to request lower volume.

After the day trip, return to Osaka for the final night.

## Day 13 - 10 May: Return

Day 13 is the return flight. The day of packing, airport transfers, leftover yen, convenience-store snacks bought for emotional support, and the quiet realization that Japan has probably ruined all future expectations of public transport.

## Final Shape

This itinerary is ambitious, but not foolish. It gives Tokyo five days, Kyoto three, Osaka three, and uses day trips smartly. There is food, culture, shopping, gaming, temples, viewpoints, markets, forests and trains. In short, the correct chaos.

The trick will be pacing. Japan rewards discipline. Wake early for the famous places. Leave space for delays. Do not underestimate train stations. Do not fight the itinerary if the body asks for mercy.

And above all, eat properly. A Japan trip without aggressive eating is just urban planning with photographs.
